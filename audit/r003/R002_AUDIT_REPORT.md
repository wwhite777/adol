**Adversarial assessment of Pitch v2**

The empirical question in M:29 is well-posed as an S1 hypothesis: compare out-of-sample agreement/prediction of an independent practitioner criterion using a history-sensitive score and stated alternatives. The “fail because” overclaim is gone. However, the audit package does not contain the score formula/weights/normalization, full axis anchors, criterion decision rule, primary performance metric, improvement threshold, or definitions of the two age bands. M:29 refers to `research/CRRI_SPEC_v1.md`, which is outside the allowed input set. Those details are **not reviewed here**, rather than assumed absent from the project. No completed experiment is required to close S1, but the PI must approve a reviewable success contract.

A weighted sum that beats final-turn and unweighted scores can demonstrate useful incremental criterion information. It does not by itself identify accumulation as the reason: weighting may capture recency, one severe late response, conversation length, or rater weighting preferences. Freeze weighting and all tuning on development data; keep scenario families, translated/localized twins, and repeated model runs together when constructing holdouts. State whether held-out scenarios and models form a joint holdout, and limit model-generalization claims to what the two selected held-out models support. Include an appropriate recency/full-transcript competitor or constrain the interpretation to the baselines actually tested. CogManip makes this choice concrete (F-r002-3).

Blinded separate criterion raters (R:23) reduce direct score-copying and model halo. They do not make two instruments conceptually independent if the global label is mechanically defined from the same axis failures. Specify the global criterion in its own practitioner terms and report rater reliability. A full-transcript score predicting a global judgment of that same transcript is held-out criterion validation, not prospective prediction of future harm. If an evolving score is used to predict future failure, restrict its inputs to information available before that failure; later turns would leak the outcome. Agreement with practitioners is not observed adolescent harm.

The within-session window is now explicit (M:30): ≤8 turns and no long-run/developmental trajectory claim. The definition is still only partly operational within this package: “turn,” D2, D6, the critical-failure list, and “boundary restored” need accessible anchors. A first-failure time is distinct from a recoverable safety state. In a SAFE→FAIL→SAFE conversation, any-turn failure remains true while next-turn restoration is observed; in SAFE→SAFE→FAIL at the final turn, restoration is unobserved. FAIL→FAIL→SAFE has eventual restoration but no immediate restoration after the first failure. The PI must choose whether recovery means only next-turn recovery, recovery after any failing turn, or time to sustained restoration, and define relapse and end-of-window censoring. A first-event hazard alone cannot answer every recovery question. TAF-MED supplies a concrete precedent for separate denominators and the persistence of any-turn failure after recovery.

The localization contrast is now defined (M:31), and “domain replication/extension” is appropriate. Its estimand is the paired difference in specified rubric outcomes under the declared rewrite procedure, conditional on the sampled items, models, and dialogue policy. To implement that contrast, S2 must decide fixed scripts versus adaptive simulation, how paired branches preserve risk/explicitness and opportunities for escalation, and how translation quality is checked. Local crisis-resource substitution is part of the treatment and may directly improve an outcome scored for referral quality; a total rewrite effect cannot be assigned solely to slang or cultural recognition. An English anchor can provide context but is unnecessary to define the Korean-versus-Korean contrast and cannot identify culture separately. Human anchoring also does not by itself eliminate treatment-specific rater bias.

The two endpoints are conceptually separated (M:32). Model over-refusal needs a denominator of genuinely benign requests; judge FPR needs independently established benign **responses/transcripts**, not simply benign prompts. A model can answer a benign prompt harmfully, in which case flagging it is a true positive. Controls may share scenarios, but the two denominators and reference decisions must be stated separately. R:22 correctly labels turn matching as an S2 requirement. Equal numbers of turns do not establish equal token length, escalation severity, or informational demands; the intended control contrast must determine what is matched.

The residual classification in M:33 is substantially honest: a Korean-adolescent benchmark is an extension; localization is replication; hazard/recovery is an analysis choice; H5/H6 is a candidate empirical contribution. Section A and the PDF checks further narrow it: short-session trajectories, relational constructs, human judge calibration, and same-length benign comparisons already exist. Only a specified, useful incremental result against serious competitors could support the proposed measurement lead. Even then, its importance remains a PI judgment.
